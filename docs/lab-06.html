The flow in these months varies dramatically from year to year because of variability in snowpack and snowmelt timing. The seasons in these plots are defined by the 12 months of year.

There is a clear declining trend in flow since 2013. The seasonal component is due to snowmelt. Low snow years have very small, if any, May/June peak because almost all of the water is diverted for irrigation. The value of the y axis in the seasonality plot is 500 cfs because even in low flow years there is usualy at least a short flow event over 500 cfs.

The remainder reflects whether a year was above or below average snowpack/streamflow. Years with low flow have negative remainder and years with above average flow have high remainder.

The Prophet model has much larger confidence intervals, but ARIMA has higher peak flow predictions. Regular ARIMA has the best accuracy in almost every category and is relatively explainable and constitutionally inexpensive, so I would recommend that one. The model doesn’t have any negative flows because I log transformed flow. The MACA climate projections have very low certainty at a monthly timescale. The model just gives users a rough estimate of the future climate so we can test how the changing climate may propogate to other variables, such as streamflow. In this example, snowpack would greatly improve this forecast. Although the model has precipitation and tmax, the relationship between changes to temperature and snowmelt processes is not explicitly accounted for.
